The core idea · the answer exhibit

An answer is a composed exhibit, not a text reply

Every reply is assembled from real metadata: a genre-colored spine, the artist's photo, prose in the archive's first-person voice, deep-link show chips, and a way into the real scene. Two of these stacked already feel like the site — because they're made of the site.

Invoking it · the cue, not just the keystroke

⌘K is invisible to the people this is for

A keyboard-only trigger would hide the feature from exactly the non-technical visitor it exists for. So the keystroke is the accelerant, never the doorway. Three tiers of cue, loudest to quietest:

Persistence · dwell-reveal, scene-deferential

Bottom-center is a contested lane — so the pill yields

5 of 6 scenes already own bottom-center (the Map stacks a nav pill + stats + hint there). A pill nailed to the screen would collide and create two competing "primary" pills. So it's present-but-deferential: collapsed to the rail dot by default, blooming bottom-center only on a scroll-pause, and only where the lane is free.

The model: standing cue = the rail's live dot (desktop) / nav "Ask" (mobile), always there for ~free. The full bar is dwell-revealed (blooms on scroll-pause, recedes on motion) and scene-deferential (collapses to the dot on contested scenes). Discoverability without permanent chrome: the dot is always visible, a one-time reveal greets first-time visitors, and the end-of-scroll card guarantees one loud exposure.

Restraint · what this deliberately is not

Where we stop, so it stays craft

Deliberately NOT:
  • ✕ A persistent bottom-right bubble following you around every scene.
  • ✕ A bot persona, avatar, or "typing…" theater. The archive has a voice, not a mascot.
  • ✕ Live-steering the canvas (flying the map, filtering the mosaic as you type) — that's the tempting over-build. Exhibits link into the scenes; they don't puppet them. Concept C, a later swing if it earns it.
  • ✕ Free-form answers ungrounded in tools — every stat in an exhibit comes from the deterministic tools, so the archive's voice never invents a number.

Pick a container (A, B, or "both, shared engine"), pick whether exhibits scroll inline or as cards — then it's ready for /spec.
