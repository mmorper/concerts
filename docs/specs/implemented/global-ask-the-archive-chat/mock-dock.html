Ask the Archive — the wayfinding dock

Reframe: the bottom-center "collision" isn't two controls fighting — it's one composed dock. A persistent Ask pill is the anchor; the scene's navigation instruction becomes its eyebrow. The pill is always there; the eyebrow adapts per scene and fades on scroll. This doesn't just park "Ask" — it gives the whole bottom lane a system it doesn't have today.

Real in this mock: tokens, genre colors, and the actual scene conflicts (Map's nav pill + stats + hint; Genres' timeline slider). Faked: backdrops.

Anatomy

One control, two tiers

The eyebrow carries the scene's voice (a hint or a nav target — small, muted). The pill is the one weighted, interactive affordance. Clear hierarchy means no "which one moves me forward?" ambiguity.

The degrade rule: where the scene's bottom content is an instruction → it becomes the eyebrow. Where it's information (Map stats) → rehome to a corner. Where it's an interactive control (Genres slider) → the dock yields entirely and the rail dot carries Ask. Honest, predictable, per-scene.

Persistence · pill stays, eyebrow breathes

The synthesis: persistent anchor, adaptive eyebrow

You wanted persistence; I worried about standing chrome. The dock resolves it: the pill never leaves (quiet, calm). The eyebrow is what responds — present when you settle, dimmed while you actively scroll, so the dock never nags during motion.

Mobile · the dock is a desktop pattern

On phones, the bottom nav already owns the floor

A persistent dock can't share the bottom edge with the fixed bottom nav. So mobile keeps the nav-pill "Ask" entry from spec #134 — tapping opens the full-screen ask page. No floating dock on small screens.

Scope · eyes open

What this actually is

This is a bottom-center navigation system, not just an Ask feature. Worth naming before /spec:
  • Touches shipped scenes. Map gets the biggest edit (rehome stats, make "tap to explore" transient). Hero/Bands are light. Genres barely changes (it yields).
  • Upside beyond Ask: the bottom lane finally gets a consistent grammar instead of five improvised treatments.
  • Desktop-first: mobile keeps the #134 nav-pill approach — one solution does not cover both.
  • Regression surface: each scene's bottom chrome + the snap-scroll nav need a pass. Higher risk than a self-contained widget — but that's the cost of coherence.

If this direction feels right, the next step is a /spec that supersedes the invocation section of #134 with this dock model — eyebrow grammar, the degrade rules, the dwell behavior, and the Spotlight bloom.
